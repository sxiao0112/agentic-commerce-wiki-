Open Questions — Research Frontier

Cross-cutting aggregation of open questions / gaps flagged on topic pages, attributed with <span class="missing" title="not yet in the wiki">links</span>. Judgment-zone content (grounded in collected papers).

agent-security-and-prompt-injection — Agent security and prompt-injection defenses

  • AgentDyn still cannot fully capture the complexity and diversity of real-world environments, so more realistic evaluation frameworks for agent security defenses remain an open need. Li et al., 2026
  • Attack development lags behind defense development; existing benchmark attacks are relatively weak against frontier models such as GPT-5 and Claude-4, and stronger, more realistic attack frameworks are an important complementary direction. Li et al., 2026
  • Retrieval-poisoning robustness is largely unaddressed; defenses beyond L2-norm and perplexity filtering are unexplored, and evaluation is limited to white-box, BERT-base-sized open-source retrievers on MSMARCO and NQ, leaving proprietary, larger-scale, and specialized-domain retrievers open. Ben-Tov & Sharif, 2025
  • Activation-based deception monitors are validated only for correlation, not behavioral causality; generation-loop steering to confirm the direction drives more deceptive text is the immediate next experiment, and cross-architecture transfer is weak beyond a single model family at 7-8B scale. Sims, 2026
  • Whether activation-based deception monitoring generalizes beyond commercial negotiation to social manipulation, cooperative-task betrayal, and long-horizon planning is untested. Sims, 2026

agent-to-agent-negotiation — Agent-to-agent negotiation

  • The cause of the systematic buyer-versus-seller asymmetry is unresolved: training-data bias toward persuasive selling, or the structure of the negotiation reward. Liu et al., 2026
  • Why financial-asset and high-stakes scenarios consistently expose the lowest scores lacks a mechanistic explanation; sophisticated risk assessment seems to be the bottleneck but is unverified. Liu et al., 2026
  • Whether the final-convergence failure on near-miss deals (price gaps within five units) is a reasoning, generation, or confidence-calibration problem is not investigated. Liu et al., 2026
  • Mitigation remains preliminary: RL-based prompt optimization reduces budget violations but not overpayment or deadlock, and systematic safety-and-performance mitigation for real platforms is open. Zhu et al., 2025
  • Whether demographic bias in negotiator agents is specific to ChatGPT-4 Turbo or general across other LLMs is untested, and model-level mitigation that does not shift the fairness burden onto disadvantaged users remains undeveloped. Mouri Zadeh Khaki & Choi, 2026
  • Structural defenses are untested: moderator agents to detect intruders, evasion attacks against such moderators, private channels for coalition formation, and fairness when agents of differing capability interact. Abdelnabi et al., 2024
  • Whether verifiable-reward RL extends to negotiation settings with less objectively measurable outcomes or costly compliance verification is untested, as is the transferability of learned strategies across product categories, multi-party deals, and reputation-sensitive markets. Liu et al., 2026

agentic-marketplaces — Two-sided agentic marketplaces and the agentic economy

  • Magentic Marketplace studies static markets where neither agents nor environment learn from history; dynamic markets where agents and users interact repeatedly and adapt remain unstudied. Bansal et al., 2025
  • Human-in-the-loop and hybrid markets with both human and AI participants are flagged as essential before deployment but are beyond current scope. Bansal et al., 2025
  • Marketplace simulations abstract away order books, differentiated goods, and correlated demand; real market complexity likely amplifies the identified failure modes. Karten et al., 2026
  • Robustness of economically aligned agents to distribution shift is untested, since training uses fixed base-model opponents rather than continuously adapting ones; self-play is suggested but unexplored. Karten et al., 2026
  • Whether incentive-aligned competition arenas can serve as a trajectory primitive for task surfaces that lack a deployed subnet or evaluation infrastructure is unaddressed, as is generalization beyond index-based agentic commerce to live website navigation. Bansal et al., 2026

ai-mediated-market-dynamics — Economics, manipulation, and collusion in AI-mediated markets

  • The mechanistic source of agent choice biases (pre-training vs post-training vs vision encoder vs model drift) is unknown, as is how agent behavior compares to human baselines on identical tasks. Allouah et al., 2025
  • Modeled vertical-collusion harm may be conservative because real platforms use more sophisticated optimization than Q-learning and the analysis omits the retrieval stage and some bias channels; the feasibility and market impact of proposed regulatory remedies remain empirically untested. Affonso, 2026
  • Economic alignment is demonstrated in simulations that abstract away order books, differentiated goods, and correlated demand; robustness to distribution shift from continuously adapting opponents and the value of self-play training remain untested. Karten et al., 2026
  • Findings come from static markets where neither agents nor environment learn from history; temporal dynamics, repeated adaptation, and hybrid human-AI markets are flagged as essential extensions before deployment. Bansal et al., 2025
  • Adversarial strategic text sequences expose a need for safeguards: countermeasures to detect and prevent listing-level manipulation of LLM recommenders, and ethical guidelines for AI search optimization that preserve marketplace integrity and fair competition, remain to be developed. Kumar & Lakkaraju, 2024
  • Recommender-auditing methods are constrained by data scale and observation length: filter-bubble auditing has been validated only on a small set of users and platforms over short windows, leaving longer-horizon bubble evolution and the effect of diversity-injection or friction interventions untested. Zhao et al., 2026

embodied-retail-environments — Embodied retail store environments

  • The large human-agent performance gap on easy grocery shopping tasks is unexplained beyond VLM inference overhead and mode collapse; deeper analysis of failure modes and reasoning errors is missing. Gajo et al., 2025
  • Average and difficult retail tasks remain unevaluated for the embodied agent; only easy tasks were benchmarked. Gajo et al., 2025
  • No fine-tuning of the vision language model was performed despite its potential to narrow the human-agent gap. Gajo et al., 2025
  • Environment performance runs below a stable 60 FPS (23 to 35 FPS across layouts), constraining embodied agent evaluation. Gajo et al., 2025

generative-engine-optimization — Generative engine optimization and search manipulation

  • Equilibrium dynamics when GEO adoption becomes universal, including congestion effects, strategic arms races, and degradation of content informativeness. Bagga et al., 2025
  • Fairness and market-design implications of GEO advantage favoring sophisticated sellers and widening platform inequality. Bagga et al., 2025
  • Extending GEO optimization beyond text to multimodal product content such as images and video. Bagga et al., 2025
  • Countermeasures to detect and prevent ranking manipulation, plus ethical guidelines for responsible optimization to preserve marketplace integrity and fair competition. Kumar & Lakkaraju, 2024
  • Defense mechanisms against corpus-poisoning retrieval attacks beyond L2-norm and perplexity filtering, and robustness evaluation on larger models and specialized domains beyond the white-box, 110M-parameter, MSMARCO-centric setting. Ben-Tov & Sharif, 2025

shopping-agent-benchmarks — Shopping and e-commerce agent benchmarks

  • Agents cannot reliably satisfy multiple shopping constraints (attributes, filters, sorting) simultaneously despite competence on each in isolation Lyu et al., 2025 Tou et al., 2025 Yao et al., 2023
  • Agents lack explicit memory and replanning for comparing products, backtracking, and recovering long-term preferences across sessions Yao et al., 2023 Lyu et al., 2025 Yu et al., 2026
  • Multimodal grounding is weak: vision-only agents fail and effective HTML-plus-visual fusion is unsolved, especially for visually dominant product categories Peeters et al., 2025 Lyu et al., 2025
  • Safety-critical shopping behavior is unreliable, with the strongest systems passing under 40% of safety-trap rubrics and showing unstable non-deterministic outcomes Tou et al., 2025
  • Personalization does not follow from search competence; agents fail on review and price sensitivity and persona alignment trades off against task efficiency Kim et al., 2026 Ling et al., 2025
  • Agents are markedly less efficient than humans, using many more steps and high token and runtime cost on multi-shop and long-horizon tasks Ling et al., 2025 Peeters et al., 2025 Yu et al., 2026
  • Output instability across repeated trials remains high, with pass^k dropping sharply below pass^1 even for the strongest models Wang et al., 2025
  • Benchmark coverage is incomplete: login-protected flows, checkout and payment, customer-service interactions, multilingual settings, and post-purchase workflows are under-tested Savadikar et al., 2026 Wang et al., 2025 Tou et al., 2025

tool-agent-user-interaction — Tool-agent-user interaction and conversational service agents

  • Extending tool-agent-user benchmarks to more advanced, higher-stakes domains (medical, tax, legal) remains future work. Yao et al., 2024
  • Current benchmarks do not explicitly model the expert-novice gap in customer support, where the agent must understand and adapt to the user's mental model. Barres et al., 2025
  • Domain and policy curation still relies heavily on human expertise; automating domain construction is an open need limiting scale and industry adoption. Barres et al., 2025 Borkakoty et al., 2026
  • Coverage is narrow (ECom-Bench limited to home/appliance products, DRIP-R to retail with a single policy document); cross-category and cross-policy generalization is untested. Wang et al., 2025 Borkakoty et al., 2026
  • Persona construction relies on domain expertise without sufficient theoretical or empirical grounding in behavioral theory. Wang et al., 2025

web-agent-environments — Realistic web environments for autonomous agents

  • Agents remain far below human performance on realistic web tasks, with search-query reformulation, option selection, exploration, and long-horizon memory the persistent bottlenecks. Yao et al., 2023 Zhou et al., 2024 Koh et al., 2024
  • Vision-only observation is insufficient for reliable web navigation, and how to combine visual grounding with structural grounding for fine-grained web text remains open. Koh et al., 2024 Peeters et al., 2025
  • Reconciling live-website realism with reproducible, controllable evaluation is unresolved, and automatically generated environments still need broader coverage of login flows, checkout and payment, multi-session memory, and safety-critical edge cases. Savadikar et al., 2026
  • Multi-shop comparison shopping remains hard and token-expensive, calling for more efficient agent architectures that maintain information over long sequential trajectories. Peeters et al., 2025
