What question does the paper ask?

Research question

Can a scalable pipeline make e-commerce test environments that retain useful structure and agent-evaluation signals from live shops, while making experiments stable, inspectable, resettable, and reproducible?

Unit of evaluation

The paper defines a task as T = (E, s0, p, V): environment E, initial state s0, natural-language intent p, and verifier V. The verifier maps an agent trajectory to binary success.

§2

Scope of the claim

ShopGym tests agents under stabilized conditions. The authors do not present it as a full replacement for live-web evaluation.

§3; Appendix C

What did the experiments compare?

Corpus

224 generated tasks across six sandbox shops. Three shops used synthetic product data. Three “twin” shops used real product data and added visual matching to their source shops.

The paper does not give task counts for each shop or bundle.

Structural study

7 real shops versus 3 synthetic shops. This comparison is explicitly unpaired. It compares accessibility-tree and state-transition graph statistics.

Therefore, it does not estimate how closely each generated shop matches one specific seed.

Behavioral study

3 real storefronts versus their 3 twins. The paper uses paired tasks and compares aggregate success-rate bars for each model, task horizon, and harness.

The paper does not report the number of paired tasks used in Figure 6.

Agent models

GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3 Flash, and GPT-5. Medium reasoning effort was used where supported. Transient API calls could be retried up to three times.

Harness A

BrowserGym supplied accessibility trees without screenshots. It allowed 20 actions and at most 30 steps. Reaching the budget counted as failure.

Harness B

The internal harness supplied a DOM/accessibility projection plus a screenshot. It allowed at most 40 steps or 850 seconds and ran three independent rollouts per task × model × environment cell.

Sources: abstract, §3, and Appendix B.

What results are supported?

Structural result

The synthetic shops had similar accessibility-tree depth, interactive-element counts, and graph node counts to the real-shop group. They had fewer edges and lower mean out-degree. The authors attribute part of that gap to excluded external links, marketing pages, and auxiliary components.

Live versus twin result

Across the bars in Figure 6, real and twin aggregate success rates were similar and moved in the same broad direction across the tested models. This is evidence that the twins retained some evaluation signal under these tasks and harnesses.

Live versus twin uncertainty

No numerical Figure 6 values, correlation coefficient, significance test, confidence interval, or paired-task denominator are reported in the paper text. Only the internal-harness panel has SEM error bars, based on three repeats.

What should readers not conclude?

No quantified correlation

“Positively correlated” is the authors’ summary of aggregate bars. The paper does not supply a coefficient or a test. Do not cite it as a measured value.

No identical failure modes

Similar aggregate pass rates do not show that live and twin shops exercise the same hidden capabilities or cause the same errors. The authors state this limit.

Proxy measures

Tree depth, action counts, and graph statistics do not fully measure visual fidelity, semantic ambiguity, content quality, or user-facing variation.

Stabilized setting

The sandboxes remove inventory churn, personalization, A/B tests, third-party services, latency variation, and other operational noise.

Task coverage

The suite excludes login, checkout and payment, recommendation and sponsored modules, customer service, cross-store comparison, post-purchase work, multi-session memory, multilingual use, and safety-critical edge cases.

Comparison limits

The structural study is unpaired and small. The behavioral study covers three twins, three models, two harnesses, and one GPT-5 judge. The paper does not report judge validation or agreement with human labels.

Appendix C: Limitations

Plain-language terms

Accessibility tree
A text-like hierarchy of page elements and their roles. An agent can use it instead of, or with, pixels.
Affordance
An action the interface makes possible, such as click, fill, or select.
Sandbox shop
A self-contained test storefront that can be reset and inspected.
Twin shop
A sandbox built with real product data and extra visual matching to one live source shop. It replaces identifying shop details with mock data.
Short horizon
A task focused on one primitive skill and relatively few steps.
Long horizon
A journey that combines several skills across more steps.
Harness
The software that shows observations to the model, executes actions, enforces budgets, and records a trajectory.
Trajectory
The ordered record of pages, observations, actions, and outcomes during one rollout.
LLM as judge
A language model that reads the task and trajectory and returns a pass or fail verdict.
