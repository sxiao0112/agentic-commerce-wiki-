Shopping Companion: Benchmarking and Training LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Preference-Grounded E-Commerce Tasks

Contribution

The paper introduces Shopping Companion Bench, a novel benchmark for evaluating and training LLM agents in cross-session preference-aware e-commerce tasks over a real-world product pool of 1.2 million items from Lazada.com. It proposes annotation-free tool-wise rewards that provide process-level supervision for memory and product tool calls, enabling lightweight 4B models to approach GPT-5 performance while addressing identified failure modes of preference hallucination and insufficient attribute verification.

Research Question

The paper addresses how LLM agents can effectively capture long-term implicit user preferences embedded across extended conversation histories and apply them to accurate product recommendation under budget and attribute constraints. Specifically: what are the main failure modes when agents handle multi-turn shopping tasks grounded in real products, and how can fine-grained process supervision improve preference grounding and multi-product coordination?

Task

Shopping Companion Bench comprises two task types. Single-product recommendation requires retrieving relevant preferences from long-term memory and finding one matching product. Add-on deal recommendation requires retrieving multiple preferences for several products while reasoning over voucher thresholds and budget constraints. Both tasks embed preference-relevant evidence sparsely as a needle within extended conversation histories (15-50 turns total) drawn from the LongMemEval corpus.

Data

The benchmark contains 1,000 instances (500 single-product, 500 add-on deals), split into 800 training and 200 test examples. Products are sourced from Lazada.com (1.29 million real-world items across broad categories: electronics, fashion, home & living, etc.), each with searchable fields including title, price, category, brand, description, and structured attributes. Instances undergo a five-step construction pipeline: product and voucher sampling; user instruction generation via LLM; synthesis of natural-language preference dialogues with automatic conflict-checking against existing user conversation history; injection into multi-topic conversation histories; and expert verification by two e-commerce annotators to ensure resolvability, searchability, and consistency. For single-product tasks, users express average 2.1 wanted attributes; for add-on deals, average 5.6 wanted attributes. User conversation histories span 56 sessions and ~106k tokens per instance, presenting a significant long-context challenge.

Metrics

Accuracy (Acc.) measures the fraction of ground-truth preference attributes correctly retrieved by the agent in Stage 1 preference identification. Success Rate (Succ.) measures the fraction of test cases satisfying all criteria: correct product count, user need satisfaction, consistency with long-term preferences, and budget feasibility (for add-on deals). Evaluation uses the LLM-as-Judge paradigm via five diverse judge models, with high inter-judge correlation (OPC = 0.9547 ± 0.0289) and strong alignment with human meta-evaluation (iPAR = 0.8950, PAR = 0.9497 with GPT-5 judge on 200 samples annotated by 8 e-commerce experts and native speakers).

Baselines

Closed-source models tested in zero-shot include GPT-5, GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, and Qwen3-Max. Open-source baselines span model families: Qwen3 (4B, 30B, 80B variants), DeepSeek-R1, Kimi-K2-Instruct, and Gemma-3-27B. GPT-5 achieves the strongest zero-shot performance (74% Acc., 64.5% Succ. average), but drops substantially on add-on deals (54% Succ.). The paper also trains Qwen3-4B with supervised fine-tuning (2,948 step-level examples via GPT-4.1 rejection sampling), outcome-level RL, and outcome-level plus tool-wise RL.

Method

The agent framework is a two-stage policy with four tools. Stage 1 (Preference Identification) uses mem_search and mem_view to retrieve and confirm user preferences from long-term dialogue history. Stage 2 (Shopping Task Execution) uses product_search and product_view to iteratively search products and verify attribute constraints against explicit requirements and inferred preferences. mem_search performs query-based retrieval over conversation history via dense vectors (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), returning top-k turns by cosine similarity. mem_view fetches full dialogue sessions by index. product_search retrieves candidates via BM25 over concatenated product titles, categories, brands, and descriptions, with optional shop ID and price-range filters, returning up to 50 results with basic metadata. product_view retrieves detailed attributes and option variants from the index. Preliminary analysis of 200 GPT-5 zero-shot trajectories reveals two-phase behavior: early memory retrieval (turns 0-2) followed by prolonged search-view loops (turns 3+). Failure attribution across 69 failed cases shows unverified attributes (39.1%), same-shop alignment failures (15.9%), and preference hallucination (10.1%) as the dominant error modes. Early errors cascade through subsequent searches, indicating that preference identification is a fragile bottleneck.

Training: Reward Design

The training objective combines three complementary reward components. Outcome-level reward (Rz) evaluates final answer quality per stage via LLM judge, scoring query relevance, matched preference-attribute count, and for add-on deals, matched product count and budget feasibility. Tool-wise process reward (Rtool) provides per-call supervision without manual annotation: for memory tools, it compares retrieved/viewed session indices against gold preference sessions; for product tools, it compares retrieved/viewed products against reference product IDs, falling back to LLM-based semantic matching when exact IDs are unavailable. Format reward (Rfmt) enforces output protocol compliance (answer tags present, thinking tags well-formed, tool-call JSON parsable, recommendation conforms to required schema). Total reward R(τ) = Rz(τz) + Rtool(τ) + Rfmt(τ). Models are trained with GRPO algorithm via VeRL framework on 800 training instances (1,600 instances after two-stage splitting) for 2.6 epochs with learning rate 1e-6, eight rollouts per sample, maximum 20 assistant turns per trajectory, temperature 0.6, top-k=20 top-p=0.95, on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs with FSDP distributed training.

Results

On single-product tasks, GPT-5 achieves 82% Acc./75% Succ.; open-source baselines range from 49-81% Acc./44-75% Succ. On add-on deals, performance drops sharply: GPT-5 66% Acc./54% Succ.; Qwen3-4B zero-shot 11% Acc./6% Succ. Fine-tuned Qwen3-4B with outcome-level RL reaches 69.5% Acc./59.5% Succ. (average). Adding tool-wise rewards achieves 72.5% Acc./63.5% Succ. (average), with 90% Acc./84% Succ. on single products and 55% Acc./43% Succ. on add-on deals, surpassing all open-source baselines and approaching GPT-5 performance. Ablation studies show tool-wise supervision increases average tool-wise score throughout training and produces shorter, more efficient trajectories: outcome-level RL alone uses 9.82 turns and 10,485 tokens average response length; outcome-level plus tool-wise RL uses 8.89 turns and 10,069 tokens. User simulator evaluation shows one-stage setting (preference identification and shopping end-to-end) achieves only 52.5% average success vs. two-stage no-hint baseline 64.5%, with low-hint and high-hint user feedback improving to 68.5% and 70.0%, indicating preference grounding remains a bottleneck even with oracle context (85% single-product, 73% add-on, 79% average).

Conclusion

The paper presents a product-grounded benchmark for evaluating shopping agents that must recover long-term user preferences from cross-session conversations and apply them to real-world recommendation tasks. Results demonstrate that this setting remains challenging even for strong LLMs: GPT-5 achieves 64.5% zero-shot success, with most failures arising from insufficient verification and tool-calling errors. The proposed annotation-free tool-wise rewards provide fine-grained supervision over memory and product tool calls, enabling a lightweight 4B agent trained on this objective to improve both preference grounding and task success, reaching 63.5% average success.
