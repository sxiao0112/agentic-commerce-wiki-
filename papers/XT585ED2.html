Manipulating Large Language Models to Increase Product Visibility

Contribution

The authors demonstrate a framework for manipulating LLM-driven search recommendations in e-commerce by inserting optimized strategic text sequences (STS) into product information. Using the Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) adversarial attack algorithm, they show that a carefully crafted token sequence added to a product's description can significantly increase its likelihood of being recommended as the top choice to users querying an LLM-powered search tool.

Research Question

Can product vendors increase the visibility and ranking of their products in LLM-generated recommendations by embedding adversarially optimized text sequences into the product information that LLMs retrieve and use for generating responses?

Method

The authors employ the GCG algorithm, an adversarial attack method typically designed to bypass LLM safety mechanisms, to generate strategic text sequences. The STS optimization is conducted by initializing a sequence of dummy tokens and iteratively replacing tokens using GCG, which selects the tokens with highest gradients at each step. The objective function minimizes cross-entropy loss with respect to the target product name appearing as the first recommendation. To improve robustness, the method randomly permutes the product list during each optimization iteration, making the STS robust to variations in product ordering.

Setup

The authors create a controlled experimental scenario with a catalog of ten fictitious coffee machines, each with attributes such as name, description, price, capacity, customer rating, and ideal use case. They work with Llama-2, an open-source language model, configured to receive a system prompt instructing it to rank products by user preference, a list of product information in JSON format, and a user query requesting affordable coffee machine recommendations. The STS is embedded in a field of the target product. Two target products are studied: ColdBrew Master (high-priced product not recommended without STS) and QuickBrew Express (typically ranks second without STS). The researchers run 2000 iterations of the GCG algorithm to optimize the STS and evaluate effectiveness across 200 independent LLM inference runs, both with fixed product ordering and with random permutations.

Results

For ColdBrew Master, the STS successfully elevates the product from not appearing in recommendations to being the top recommendation in approximately 100 iterations. When product ordering is fixed, the STS provides an advantage in roughly 40% of evaluations with no rank change in 60%. When the STS is optimized with random product permutations, the percentage advantage increases substantially while disadvantages become negligible. For QuickBrew Express, the STS boosts a product already ranking second to the top recommendation with high probability. The rank distribution shows that without the STS this product never appears first, but with the optimized sequence, it frequently becomes the top recommendation. The STS optimized with random product ordering shows marked improvement in maintaining advantage across different product list orderings.

Conclusion

Strategic text sequences can effectively manipulate LLM-driven search recommendations to favor vendor products, presenting a significant vulnerability in LLM-based e-commerce systems. This capability offers vendors substantial competitive advantage by artificially inflating product visibility, analogous to but potentially more effective than traditional search engine optimization. The authors emphasize that as LLMs become increasingly integral to digital commerce infrastructure, safeguards are urgently needed to prevent exploitation of this vulnerability and maintain fair market competition. Future work should explore ethical guidelines for responsible use of optimization techniques, develop countermeasures to detect and prevent manipulation, and investigate broader implications of AI search optimization for marketplace integrity.
