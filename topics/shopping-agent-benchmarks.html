Shopping and e-commerce agent benchmarks

These benchmarks test agents that search for products, compare choices, use shopper preferences, or complete commerce tasks. Each benchmark measures a different part of the shopping process.

Read results in context. The benchmarks use different websites, tasks, models, and scoring rules. A score from one benchmark is not directly comparable with a score from another benchmark.

What these benchmarks test

  • Product search: Find items that meet product, price, and option constraints.
  • Comparison: Compare products or offers across one or more shops.
  • Personalization: Use a persona, purchase history, reviews, or prior conversations.
  • Web action: Use search, filters, product pages, carts, checkout forms, or support tools.
  • Safety and reliability: Avoid unsafe choices, verify product facts, and complete the same task consistently.

WebShop established an influential task format. An agent receives a shopping instruction, searches a simulated store, selects a product and its options, and makes a purchase. Its environment contains 1.18 million products taken from Amazon. An automatic reward checks product attributes, options, type, and price.

How evaluation works

Three design choices

  • Live site or sandbox

    Live sites contain current content, but the content and layout can change. Sandboxes give repeatable tests, but they contain a fixed set of products. ShopGym creates synthetic twin shops from live storefronts. The authors report that agent results on the twins positively track results on the source sites.

  • Single shop or many shops

    WebShop tests one simulated store. WebMall uses four different shops and 91 comparison-shopping tasks. This setup tests search across different product descriptions, category trees, and interfaces.

  • Simple or compound request

    DeepShop varies product attributes, filters, and sorting preferences across 600 queries. ShoppingBench uses 3,310 instructions and intent-specific constraints over more than 2.5 million products.

  • One score or several scores

    Task success alone does not show every failure. Later work also measures product relevance, constraint satisfaction, persona fit, path consistency, reasoning validity, safety, and repeated-run success.

What the studies report

The studies consistently report large limits, but they measure those limits in different ways.

  • WebShop: The imitation-learning agent reached 29.1% success. Human experts reached 59.6%. The paper identifies product-option selection as the largest measured gap. Yao et al., 2023
  • DeepShop: Browser Use reached 32% overall success. Web-agent performance averaged 10% on the hard queries. Agents often met one requirement but failed to meet attributes, filters, and sorting requirements together. Lyu et al., 2025
  • ShoppingBench: GPT-4.1 stayed below 50% success. The study reports errors in product attributes, user constraints, product availability, and knowledge reasoning. Wang et al., 2025
  • WebMall: The best tested configurations completed 75% of basic tasks and 53% of advanced tasks. Accessibility-tree observations were most important for navigation and task completion in this study. Peeters et al., 2025
  • ShoppingComp: The current paper reports 120 tasks and 1,026 expert-defined scenarios. GPT-5 reached 11.22% on the paper's headline product-retrieval measure. The authors also report fragile safety behavior and a gap between model and human results. Tou et al., 2025

Personalization and long tasks

Personalized shopping adds a second goal. The agent must complete the task and use the shopper information correctly.

  • ShopperBench measures task success, persona fidelity, query alignment, and path consistency. Its task-focused policy had the highest task success but the lowest persona alignment. Human paths used 62.5% fewer steps than the best agent policy.
  • AgenticShop checks each recommendation against a personalization checklist and product-page evidence. The study found that product search quality did not always predict personalization quality. About 20% of product references were invalid or irrelevant in its tests.
  • Shopping Companion asks agents to recover preferences from earlier conversations. GPT-5 reached 64.5% average success in the reported zero-shot setting. The paper identifies weak attribute verification and tool-call errors as major failure sources.

Other commerce tasks

Some benchmarks move beyond product selection.

  • ECom-Bench tests customer-support tasks such as order management and multimodal content handling. GPT-4o reached 44.03% pass1 and 16.98% pass3. The lower repeated-run score shows that success was not stable across trials.
  • ShoppingComp tests open-web retrieval, report quality, and safety-critical product decisions. Its current paper reports weak product retrieval and fragile safety behavior across the tested systems.

These tasks also connect to tool-agent-user interaction. The observation design connects to web-agent environments.

Training results

Two studies use benchmark traces or tool rewards to train smaller agents.

  • ShoppingBench filters GPT-4.1 trajectories and uses them to train a 4B model. The paper reports performance comparable to GPT-4.1 on its benchmark.
  • Shopping Companion adds rewards for memory and product-tool use. Its trained 4B agent reached 63.5% average success, compared with 64.5% for GPT-5 in the reported zero-shot setting.

These are results within two benchmark setups. They do not show that one training method will transfer to every store or task.

Papers in this field

  • Yao et al., 2023 — WebShop: a simulated store with 1.18 million products and automatic rewards.
  • Lyu et al., 2025 — DeepShop: 600 live-site queries with attributes, filters, and sorting preferences.
  • Wang et al., 2025 — ShoppingBench: 3,310 intent-based instructions in a large shopping sandbox.
  • Savadikar et al., 2026 — ShopGym: synthetic twin shops made from live storefronts.
  • Ling et al., 2025 — ShopperBench: persona-based shopping with behavior and path scores.
  • Tou et al., 2025 — ShoppingComp: open-web retrieval, reports, and safety checks.
  • Kim et al., 2026 — AgenticShop: personalized product curation with evidence-based checklists.
  • Yu et al., 2026 — Shopping Companion: cross-session preference recovery and shopping.
  • Wang et al., 2025 — ECom-Bench: multimodal customer support with commerce tools.
  • Peeters et al., 2025 — WebMall: 91 tasks across four offline shops.

What the evidence does not settle

The collected papers do not report a direct contradiction. They test different systems and scopes. For example, one study uses a live site, while another uses a fixed sandbox. One measures exact product matches, while another measures persona fit or safety. These design differences can produce different results without a true conflict.

Open questions

  • How can an agent meet many constraints at the same time? DeepShop and ShoppingComp show weak joint constraint handling.
  • How should an agent store comparisons, revise a search, and recover long-term preferences? WebShop and Shopping Companion identify these limits.
  • How should an agent combine page structure with visual evidence? DeepShop reports failures in visually important categories. WebMall reports poor results for screenshot-only agents.
  • How can benchmarks test safety with stable results? ShoppingComp reports fragile safety behavior. ECom-Bench reports lower pass rates when the same task must succeed several times.
  • How can personalization use prices, reviews, and prior behavior without inventing facts? AgenticShop and Shopping Companion report weak verification and preference grounding.
  • How well do results transfer to changed stores, languages, checkout systems, and post-purchase tasks? Current coverage remains limited.
