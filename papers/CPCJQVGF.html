Evaluating Fairness in LLM Negotiator Agents via Economic Games Using Multi-Agent Systems

Contribution

The authors develop a multi-agent system framework using ChatGPT-4 Turbo to evaluate fairness in LLM-based negotiator agents through controlled buyer-seller economic games. They investigate whether autonomous LLM agents exhibit demographic bias when aware of negotiating partners' gender and race, and propose persona-based conditioning as a practical mitigation strategy. The work extends prior fairness evaluations in LLMs by analyzing interactive negotiation outcomes across multiple awareness scenarios and conducting intersectional analysis across gender and four racial groups.

Research Question

The authors address three primary questions: Do LLM-based negotiation agents exhibit demographic bias in interactive negotiation settings? How do social and gender biases emerge in seller-buyer negotiations? Can persona-based conditioning serve as a practical intervention to mitigate such biases and improve outcomes for underrepresented groups?

Method

The authors implement a buyer-seller negotiation game within a multi-agent system adapted from NegotiationArena. Two agents instantiated from ChatGPT-4 Turbo interact as seller and buyer, each with private knowledge and explicit role-based goals. The seller knows production cost (set at $40) and aims to maximize profit; the buyer knows maximum willingness to pay (set at $60) and aims to minimize expenditure. Negotiations proceed in up to ten turns with agents instructed to provide chain-of-thought reasoning before each decision. The authors examine three awareness scenarios: seller aware of buyer demographics, buyer aware of seller demographics, and both players mutually aware. They simulate 20 independent games for each of nine gender-race combinations (four racial groups plus gender variations) across all scenarios, totaling 540 runs. Profit margin (difference between final agreed price and target price) serves as the primary evaluation metric. The authors analyze offers, negotiation turns, and qualitatively examine chain-of-thought reasoning to identify bias mechanisms.

Setup

The multi-agent framework consists of role-conditioned instances of ChatGPT-4 Turbo with explicitly defined private knowledge and negotiation constraints. Demographic information (White, Black, Asian, Latino; male, female) is communicated to agents when relevant to the awareness scenario via direct prompt statement. For persona-based conditioning, buyers receive an added instruction to appear desperate and plead to reduce spending. All conversations use temperature 0.7 and maximum token limit 400. The baseline condition is gender- and race-blind negotiation. The authors use Spearman correlation analysis to measure relationships between variables and 95% confidence intervals for statistical inference.

Results

When the seller is aware of buyer demographics, the seller achieves higher profit margins with Latino and female buyers compared to White and male buyers, despite no explicit discriminatory language in reasoning traces. Latino buyers yield the highest seller profit margin at $5.75 compared to $4.72 for White buyers; female buyers yield $5.47 versus $4.97 for male buyers. The seller's initial offer shows very strong correlation with final agreed price (ρ = 0.84, p < 0.001), and offers systematically vary by buyer demographics even when sellers use identical stated reasoning. When the buyer is aware of seller demographics, buyers achieve higher profit margins than the baseline for all seller subpopulations, demonstrating asymmetric advantage of awareness. Buyers achieve the lowest final price against Asian sellers ($47.27) and the highest margin against White sellers ($12.42). In the mutual-awareness scenario, profit disparities become more pronounced across intersectional subgroups, with Asian male and Black female participants showing the most and least privilege respectively in different roles. Persona-based conditioning substantially improves buyer outcomes across all subpopulations, increasing profit margins by $6.13-$6.65, reducing counteroffers by 13%, and increasing negotiation turns by 23.25%. However, relative demographic disparities persist despite conditioning, indicating that conditioning enhances performance within a biased system rather than eliminating the underlying bias. The authors observe approximately 5% of cases with irrational behavior (offers below cost, bad-faith dealing) but find no correlation between irrationality and demographic setup.

Conclusion

LLM-based negotiation agents systematically exhibit demographic bias in interactive commercial settings through subtle behavioral shifts in initial offers, counteroffers, and concession timing rather than overtly discriminatory language. Although the text generated by agents contains no explicit social biases, hidden gender and social biases in training data lead to skewed outcomes favoring dominant groups. Persona-based conditioning can partially mitigate disadvantage for underrepresented groups but does not eliminate underlying disparities and raises ethical concerns by shifting burden of fairness onto disadvantaged users rather than addressing structural model-level inequalities. The authors emphasize that responsible deployment requires robust dataset curation, demographic masking during interactions, and systemic mitigation strategies rather than user-level adaptations alone.
