Tool-agent-user interaction and conversational service agents

Benchmarks for language agents that hold multi-turn conversations with users, call programmatic tools and APIs, and follow domain policies while resolving customer-service and retail tasks.

Definition

Tool-agent-user interaction names a setting where a language agent must do three things at once: talk with a human user over multiple turns, call programmatic APIs, and adhere to domain-specific policies. Yao et al., 2024 introduces this framing through τ-bench and formulates each task as a partially observable Markov decision process whose state combines a database and a user state. The user instruction is hidden from the agent, so the agent must gather information through conversation rather than receive it upfront. Success is judged objectively by comparing the final database state to an annotated ground-truth outcome, and by checking that the agent conveys the required information back to the user.

A central concept from Yao et al., 2024 is reliability rather than discovery. The pass^k metric measures the probability that all k independent trials succeed, capturing whether an agent behaves consistently across variations in conversation flow. This is distinct from pass@k, which measures whether at least one trial succeeds.

Barres et al., 2025 extends this setting to a dual-control environment where both the agent and the simulated user can call tools and modify a shared world state. Where the τ-bench user is passive and supplies information only, the dual-control user possesses its own tool affordances. The interaction is modeled as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision process, and this formalism isolates communication and coordination as a bottleneck separate from pure reasoning.

Wang et al., 2025 frames the same interaction for e-commerce customer support, adding multimodal content and persona-driven users. Agents are evaluated with Model Context Protocol tools and must handle product inquiries, order management, returns, and image-based tasks. The persona profiles encode emotion, patience, trust, and communication style, grounding the simulated user in consumer-behavior characteristics drawn from real customer interactions.

Borkakoty et al., 2026 adds policy ambiguity as a defining concept. The authors draw on value pluralism, the premise that conflicting legitimate values can admit no universally correct resolution, and define policy ambiguity as the coexistence of multiple valid interpretations of a policy clause. Under such ambiguity, the benchmark accepts that no single correct outcome may exist for a customer-service task.

State of the area (synthesis)

The four collected papers form a single line of work that pushes the tool-agent-user setting toward greater realism, one dimension at a time. Yao et al., 2024 establishes the base: a modular environment of databases, domain APIs, policy documents, and a language-model user simulator, evaluated by database-state comparison. Its headline result is that even strong function-calling agents like gpt-4o succeed on under half of the airline tasks, and that pass^k drops sharply as k grows, exposing poor consistency. The same paper finds that removing domain policies hurts airline tasks far more than retail, suggesting agents struggle most with complex ad-hoc rules.

Barres et al., 2025 keeps the retail and airline domains from τ-bench and adds a telecom domain with a compositional task generator that builds verifiable tasks from atomic components. Its most informative result comes from ablations. A no-user autonomous mode isolates reasoning and scores far higher than the default; an oracle-plan mode that supplies the ground-truth action sequence scores higher still. The gap between these modes and full dual-control operation, roughly a 20% pass^1 penalty, locates the difficulty in coordination and communication rather than reasoning. Performance also collapses as the required action count grows, with near-zero pass rates beyond seven actions.

Wang et al., 2025 moves the setting into e-commerce customer support and adds two things the earlier benchmarks lacked: multimodal tasks and personas built from real customer behavior. It reports the same reliability problem from a different domain. GPT-4o leads at 44% pass^1 but falls steeply at higher k, and a decoupled approach that treats a multimodal model as a tool beats an end-to-end multimodal planner. The persona findings reinforce a thread running through this area: simulated-user characteristics such as emotional state, patience, and communication style materially shift agent behavior, so user simulation is not a neutral backdrop but part of what is being measured.

Borkakoty et al., 2026 targets the policy layer that the earlier benchmarks held simple. Where τ-bench and τ²-bench use purpose-built, unambiguous policies, DRIP-R grounds its tasks in Amazon's real return policy and deliberately builds in ambiguous clauses. Its central finding is disagreement: across four frontier models, only 4.2% of scenarios produce a fully shared outcome, while 13.3% produce four different outcomes. This recasts low benchmark scores in a new light. Some of the inconsistency that τ-bench measured as unreliability may, under genuinely ambiguous policy, reflect an absence of any single correct answer rather than a model failure.

Several methods recur across all four papers and mark the shared toolkit of this area. The pass^k consistency metric introduced by Yao et al., 2024 is adopted by Barres et al., 2025 and Wang et al., 2025. Database-state comparison is the common backbone of evaluation, augmented with information-retrieval and output checks. The Dec-POMDP dual-control formalism of Barres et al., 2025 is reused by Borkakoty et al., 2026, which gives the agent tool access while the user simulator has none. Language-model user simulators are universal, though their fidelity is a known weak point: Barres et al., 2025 reports its telecom user simulator errs only 16% of the time against 40 to 47% in the retail and airline domains it inherits.

The collected error analyses converge on a shared failure taxonomy. Yao et al., 2024 and Wang et al., 2025 both separate wrong arguments, wrong decisions, and partial resolution of compound requests. Borkakoty et al., 2026 adds a behavior-decision gap, finding that agents' in-conversation policy adherence exceeds their final resolution adherence, largest under coordination ambiguity. Taken together, the area shows that current agents can reason in isolation but falter when they must coordinate with an active user, follow non-trivial rules, and stay consistent across runs. This setting is the conversational, policy-bound counterpart to the task-completion focus of shopping-agent-benchmarks and the page-navigation focus of web-agent-environments, both of which share WebShop and WebArena as common reference points.

Papers in this field

  • Yao et al., 2024 — τ-bench: a benchmark measuring whether agents can interact consistently with simulated users and programmatic APIs while following domain-specific policies, with a pass^k metric for reliability.
  • Barres et al., 2025 — τ²-bench: a dual-control benchmark where both the agent and the simulated user can call tools and modify a shared state, isolating coordination and communication as bottlenecks.
  • Wang et al., 2025 — ECom-Bench: the first open-source framework for evaluating multimodal LLM agents with MCP tools on real-world e-commerce customer-support issues using persona-driven user simulation.
  • Borkakoty et al., 2026 — DRIP-R: a benchmark for LLM conversational agents operating under realistic, ambiguous retail policies, with multi-dimensional evaluation and evidence of cross-model disagreement on resolutions.

Contradictions

  • Whether benchmark policies should be simplified or ambiguous. Yao et al., 2024 and Barres et al., 2025 evaluate agents against purpose-built, unambiguous policies and treat inconsistent outcomes as agent unreliability. Borkakoty et al., 2026 argues this misses an inherent feature of real-world deployment, grounds its tasks in real ambiguous policy, and shows frontier models fundamentally disagree on resolutions where no single correct outcome exists, reframing some apparent unreliability as irreducible decision ambiguity.

Open questions / gaps

  • Extending these benchmarks to more advanced, higher-stakes domains such as medical, tax, and legal remains future work Yao et al., 2024. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Current benchmarks do not explicitly model the expert-novice gap in customer support, where the agent must understand and adapt to the user's mental model Barres et al., 2025. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Domain and policy curation still relies heavily on human expertise, limiting scale and industry adoption; automating domain construction is an open need Barres et al., 2025 Borkakoty et al., 2026. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Coverage is narrow: ECom-Bench is confined to home and appliance products and DRIP-R to retail with a single primary policy document; cross-category and cross-policy generalization is untested Wang et al., 2025 Borkakoty et al., 2026. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Persona construction relies on domain expertise without sufficient theoretical or empirical grounding in behavioral theory Wang et al., 2025. Mirrored in open-questions.
