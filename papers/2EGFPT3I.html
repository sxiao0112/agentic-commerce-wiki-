AgenticShop: Benchmarking Agentic Product Curation for Personalized Web Shopping

Contribution

AgenticShop is the first benchmark designed to evaluate agentic systems on personalized product curation in open web shopping environments. The benchmark addresses a gap in existing e-commerce benchmarks by introducing three key features: realistic shopping intents derived from user behavior (Target Finding, Alternative Selection, Open Exploration), diverse user profiles constructed from real purchase histories and review texts, and a checklist-driven personalization evaluation framework grounded in verifiable product information. The work demonstrates through comprehensive experiments that current agentic systems remain insufficient for effective personalized product curation across the open web.

Research Question

How can agentic systems be rigorously evaluated on their ability to curate products that align with diverse user preferences in realistic open web shopping scenarios? Specifically, how well do search-augmented LLMs and autonomous web agents perform when tasked with integrating dispersed product information across multiple platforms and delivering personalized recommendations grounded in fine-grained user preferences?

Task

AgenticShop formulates personalized product curation as an open web task where an agentic system receives a user shopping profile describing preferences and constraints, a query reflecting the user's current shopping intent, and must explore the open web to generate a list of curated products matching the user's personalization requirements. The benchmark distinguishes three shopping intents: Target Finding (users seek a specific product with varying query specificity levels), Alternative Selection (users want meaningful comparisons across options with varying product domain involvement levels), and Open Exploration (users casually browse without explicit queries, requiring proactive curation across domains).

Data

The benchmark is constructed from the Amazon Review dataset, systematically sampling users and products across domains. The authors select users with at least 100 reviews to ensure sufficient interaction history, identify each user's core shopping interest through their most frequently purchased subcategories, and create focused evaluation scenarios. Four domains are used: Grocery & Gourmet Food for Target Finding scenarios, Home & Kitchen and Electronics for Alternative Selection low-involvement and high-involvement scenarios respectively, Clothing Shoes & Jewelry for aesthetic-driven scenarios, and all domains combined for Open Exploration. A total of 350 personalized tasks are created with 50 user profiles per shopping scenario (350 tasks total), each with distinct product types. User profiles consist of three components: narrative descriptions of shopping preferences generated by an LLM from purchase history and reviews, intent-specific queries tailored to one of the three shopping intents, and checklists categorizing user requirements into evaluable attributes spanning brand preferences, price sensitivity, review sensitivity, functional requirements, aesthetic preferences, and purchase expectations.

Metrics

The evaluation framework employs an LLM-as-a-judge approach grounded in verifiable product information. For each curated product, the system extracts all available information from product pages including brand, price, descriptions, delivery options, and reviews using multimodal information extraction (URLs and screenshots from fully rendered webpages). The judge then performs binary verification for each checklist criterion, determining whether a product satisfies each requirement. The overall curation score is computed as the average proportion of satisfied checklist criteria across all users, products, and checklist items. The authors also evaluate search capability independently (hit ratio metric), checking whether agents locate relevant products regardless of personalization alignment.

Baselines

The benchmark evaluates eight agentic systems: four search-augmented LLMs (ChatGPT Search, Claude Sonnet 4 Search, Gemini-2.5 flash-grounding, Perplexity-pro) that generate responses by incorporating information retrieved through web search tools, and four autonomous web agents (Agent-E, SeeAct, Web Voyager, Browser Use) that directly interact with live web interfaces through browsing and navigation.

Results

Current agentic systems show modest performance on personalized product curation. Among search-augmented LLMs, ChatGPT Search and Claude Sonnet 4 Search achieve the strongest results at around 30-35% across scenarios, while Gemini-2.5 flash-grounding and Perplexity-pro perform considerably weaker. Autonomous web agents show mixed results, with SeeAct and Web Voyager performing comparably to the better search-augmented LLMs on all cases, but Agent-E showing significantly weaker capabilities. Open Exploration poses the greatest challenge with all models showing substantially lower performance due to the need to balance relevance with novelty across diverse domains without explicit user queries. Query specificity contributes marginally to personalization, with only slight improvements when queries include more detailed specifications, suggesting that the open web's lack of structured taxonomies limits the benefit of refined queries. Performance varies significantly across product domains, with Electronics (high-involvement) posing greater challenges than Home & Kitchen (low-involvement), while Fashion (aesthetic-driven) presents the most severe performance degradation. Hit ratio analysis reveals that good product search capability does not necessarily translate into effective personalization, indicating bottlenecks beyond mere product discovery. Attribution errors persist across all systems, with approximately 20% of product references hallucinated as invalid URLs or irrelevant pages. When excluding failure cases, autonomous web agents outperform search-augmented LLMs, suggesting their iterative exploration mechanisms provide advantages on successful runs. Performance analysis across checklist dimensions reveals that systems effectively address brand preferences but exhibit significant limitations in capturing review sensitivity and price sensitivity, primarily due to difficulty locating review-rich products and aggregating scattered pricing information across variants and purchase options.

Conclusion

AgenticShop establishes a foundation for developing user-side shopping agents capable of personalized product curation by introducing the first comprehensive benchmark that evaluates agentic systems on this task. The work reveals that current agentic systems struggle with personalized product curation, emphasizing the need for advances in exploratory information seeking and alternative comparison. The finding that alternative evaluation strongly correlates with personalization performance in search-augmented LLMs, while exploration contributes more to autonomous web agents' personalization, suggests that balancing exploration with systematic alternative comparison offers a promising direction for future agentic systems designed for e-commerce applications.

Related Work

Web-based Agentic Systems

Search-augmented LLMs — WebGPT, WebGLM, Perplexity Search — aim at improving question answering by retrieving and synthesizing evidence from the web, as noted by the authors to represent early efforts that integrated web access to expand LLM capabilities. Autonomous web agents represent another line of research focusing on building systems capable of solving highly interactive tasks, progressing from text-based approaches to multimodal paradigms like those demonstrated in recent studies. Web-based agentic systems represent a natural application domain to e-commerce, yet the authors note their application to real-world shopping remains underexplored. Operator System represents recent autonomous agent systems. Recent multimodal web agents including Web Voyager, Agent-E, and SeeAct are evaluated in the benchmark.

Agent Benchmarks

Static environment benchmarks — WebShop, Mind2Web — progressed toward reproducible sandbox environments like VisualWebArena and Weblinx, followed by more recent real-world live website benchmarks like Mind2Web 2, WebCanvas, and WebArena. E-commerce specific benchmarks include WebShop (product search task on simulated environment), ShoppingBench (purchase optimization such as discounts and free shipping), WebMall (comparison shopping across simulated websites), DeepShop (search with attribute filters and sorting), AmazonBench (account management and wishlist operations), ChatShop (interactive information seeking), and MMLU shopping tasks. Compared to existing e-commerce benchmarks, AgenticShop uniquely combines open web product curation evaluation with explicit personalization grounding. PersonalWAB introduces personalization to web agents but without extending this to the evaluation stage. The authors identify that existing benchmarks fall short by reducing evaluation to single-platform lookups or predefined items, failing to test discovery across diverse sources and remaining confined within predetermined platforms. ChineseEcomQA and the Amazon-M2 multilingual shopping dataset represent related work in e-commerce evaluation, though focused on recommendation and text generation rather than agentic product curation.

Evaluation of Agentic Systems

Early evaluation approaches relied on manual human assessment for web agent performance, but limited scalability drove development of automated methods. Checkpoint-based evaluation combined rule-based functions with LLM-as-a-judge for automated assessment, as demonstrated in prior studies. WebJudge advanced toward fully human-agnostic evaluation by leveraging LLMs to identify key checkpoints and screenshots. DeepShop prompted LLMs to assess whether outcomes match query requirements. The authors note that evaluating e-commerce systems presents unique challenges as task outcomes are shaped by user-specific preferences and constraints. Their checklist-driven personalization evaluation framework addresses this gap by grounding LLM-as-a-judge decisions in verifiable extracted product information, providing fine-grained and scalable assessment of user preference satisfaction. Consumer decision-making models informed the analysis of agent behaviors (information seeking, alternative evaluation, purchase decision) and their correlation with personalization performance.

E-commerce Information Seeking and Recommendations

Product search and recommendation systems have predominantly focused on improving product discovery within individual platforms, as noted by the authors. Studies on e-commerce search indicate that web shopping is inherently exploratory, with users consulting external sources, comparing alternatives across multiple platforms, and making purchase decisions based on complex price and offering evaluations. User intent, behavior, and perceived satisfaction in product search were studied to understand decision-making processes. Information overload in e-commerce environments and its effects on consumer return intentions and purchase decisions are documented challenges. User orientations and stage-specific behaviors in exploratory search provide formative understanding of how users navigate shopping tasks. Supporting keyword search in product databases through probabilistic approaches and learning latent vector spaces for product search represent prior technical work in this area. Recent approaches include contrastive learning for user sequence representation in personalized product search and neural information retrieval approaches combining ranking models with graph embeddings. The shift toward personalized conversational sales agents and conversational recommender systems represents emerging directions in e-commerce technology.
