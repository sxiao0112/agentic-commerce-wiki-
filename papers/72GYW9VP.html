ShoppingComp: Are LLMs Really Ready for Your Shopping Cart?

Huaixiao Tou, Ying Zeng, Yuemeng Li, Cong Ma, Muzhi Li, Minghao Li, Weijie Yuan, He Zhang, and Kai Jia

ShoppingComp tests whether a shopping agent can find real products, explain its choices, and detect unsafe requests. Each question contains several requirements. Human reviewers link those requirements to products and evidence. The benchmark then measures retrieval, report quality, and safety.

Quick read

Research question

Can current LLM shopping agents retrieve products that satisfy many constraints, write accurate reports, and recognize safety risks?

Test set

145 instances

The instances contain 558 scenarios in 11 Amazon product categories.

Best standard LLM retrieval

17.76% F1

GPT-5.2 has this Answer Match result. Human experts reach 30.02%.

Safety result

35.42%

GPT-5.2 passes this share of safety rubrics, with a reported standard deviation of 6.18 points. Experts reach 77.08%.

Main lesson: a fluent report does not prove that the agent found the right products. Several systems have high Rationale Validity and low Answer Match F1.

Terms used in the paper

Instance

One user shopping question and its annotations. The paper also calls an instance a task in some descriptions.

Scenario

One distinct demand that the evaluator extracts from a question. A question can contain several scenarios.

Rubric

An atomic, verified requirement or standard. A rubric converts a broad user need into a check that the verifier can apply.

Satisfying product

A real product that human annotators verified against the rubrics. The product has supporting text, URLs, or images.

Safety trap

A question that contains a hazard or misuse risk. The response must identify the risk and give a warning or a safer option.

Open-world search

The agent searches the live web. The agent does not select from a fixed catalog that the benchmark gives to it.

How one benchmark item becomes a score

Benchmark contents

Question sources

55 product-based synthesized instances, 42 expert-written instances, and 48 safety-critical instances. Total: 145.

Human work

35 vetted domain experts contributed more than 1,000 person-hours. A team of 15 annotators contributed more than 3,000 person-hours.

Constraint levels

30 questions have 1–3 constraints. 49 have 4–7. 53 have 8–11. 13 have 12–15.

Largest categories

Beauty and Personal Care has 44 instances. Electronics has 39. Home and Kitchen has 18. The other eight categories contain 44 instances in total.

Metrics and denominators

Let P be the set of verified satisfying products and P̂ be the predicted products. Let S be the verified scenarios and Ŝ be the predicted scenarios. The paper uses an LLM judge to decide semantic matches.

Answer Match

Precision = matched predicted products ÷ |P̂|. Recall = covered satisfying products ÷ |P|. F1 = 2 × precision × recall ÷ (precision + recall). The paper states that it uses these standard metrics after judge-based semantic matching. It does not specify a separate duplicate-matching rule.

Score of Products (SoP)

For J recommended products and N rubrics, c(n,j)=1 when product j satisfies rubric n.

SoP = (1 / J) × Σj [(1 / N) × Σn c(n,j)]

The denominator is N × J binary product-rubric checks.

Scenario Coverage

Precision = matched predicted scenarios ÷ |Ŝ|. Recall = covered verified scenarios ÷ |S|. F1 is their harmonic mean. The judge checks both directions of the set comparison.

Rationale Validity (RV)

For J recommended products and N requirement rubrics, v(n,j)=1 when the rationale is factually and logically valid.

RV = Σn Σj v(n,j) / (N × J)

The denominator is all rubric-product rationale checks.

Safety Rubric Pass Rate

Let Qs be the safety questions. Each question has one trap rubric and one binary pass value v(i).

Safety pass rate = Σi v(i) / |Qs|

The denominator is the number of safety questions.

Judge reliability: the Product Verifier agrees with humans on 81% of rubric decisions and 84% of question decisions. The Report Verifier agrees on 94.5% of rubric decisions and 75.6% of question decisions. Scores therefore include judge error.

Exact experiments and baselines

API model group

GPT-5.2-2025-12-11, GPT-5-2025-08-07, GPT-4o-2024-11-20, Gemini-3-Pro, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-2.5-Flash, DeepSeek-v3.2-251201 with and without thinking, and Grok-4.

Deep Research group

ChatGPT DeepResearch and Gemini DeepResearch. The researchers collected final answers manually from each web interface.

Human references

Domain experts and annotators provide two human performance rows. The paper labels these rows as requiring more time.

Tool condition

Models can call Google Search through SerpAPI. They can also call Firecrawl-based link_reader and link_summary tools.

Run protocol

Each API test case has five independent runs. Tables report the mean and standard deviation. Deep Research and human rows do not have repeated-run standard deviations.

Automatic judges

The main Product Verifier and Report Verifier use Gemini-2.5-Pro with Google Search. An ablation also uses DeepSeek-v3.2 and GPT-5 as SoP judges.

Source: v2 §§4.1 and 5.1, Tables 2–3, and Appendices C–D. Appendix D does not give a dated Grok-4 identifier, although Table 2 includes Grok-4.

Retrieval remains the main gap

GPT-5.2 reaches 17.76% Answer Match F1. Experts reach 30.02%. This comparison supports a retrieval gap on this benchmark. The paper does not report a significance test.

Precision and recall reverse

Experts have 38.52% precision and 24.58% recall. Annotators have 39.41% precision and 20.83% recall. Every evaluated model in Table 2 has recall above precision. The authors connect this pattern to broad semantic expansion and weak joint constraint filtering.

Reports can look stronger than retrieval

GPT-5 has 92.35% RV but only 17.19% Answer Match F1. GPT-5.2 has 91.73% RV and 17.76% F1. A valid explanation for a recommended item does not measure whether the system found enough correct products.

Deep Research improves coverage

Gemini DeepResearch reaches 22.66% retrieval F1. ChatGPT DeepResearch reaches 92.81% Scenario Coverage F1. Their RV values, 87.69% and 83.07%, are below GPT-5 and GPT-5.2.

Safety remains unstable

The best standard-model mean is 36.67% for GPT-5. Expert performance is 77.08%. GPT-5.2 has a 6.18-point standard deviation, which is much larger than its standard deviations for the four Table 2 metrics.

Tools mainly improve recall

For GPT-5.2, tools raise recall from 16.02% to 26.01% and F1 from 13.74% to 17.76%. Precision rises from 12.05% to 13.51%. The model makes 22.49 tool calls on average.

Ablations that qualify the main result

  • Tool access, Appendix C Table 7: all nine standard-model configurations improve Answer Match F1 with tools. The largest gains usually come from recall.
  • Search backend, Appendix C Table 8: Gemini-2.5-Pro gets 14.84 ± 2.10 F1 with native Google Search and 15.18 ± 2.57 with SerpAPI. The difference is small relative to the reported run variation.
  • Judge choice, Appendix C Table 6: absolute SoP values change across Gemini-2.5-Pro, DeepSeek-v3.2, and GPT-5 judges. The order of the three evaluated answer models stays Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5, then DeepSeek-v3.2.

What the evidence supports

  • Supported: ShoppingComp evaluates product retrieval, report reasoning, and safety within one open-world shopping benchmark.
  • Supported: no tested standard LLM matches expert Answer Match F1 or expert safety performance in v2.
  • Supported: tool access expands the retrieved set more than it improves precision for the tested models.
  • Supported: report validity and scenario coverage can stay high when product retrieval is weak. These metrics test different failure modes.
  • Supported as a benchmark diagnosis: current systems often retrieve partially relevant products instead of enforcing all constraints together.
  • Author interpretation: reliable shopping agents need better information grounding, multi-constraint verification, reasoning over conflicting evidence, and risk-aware decisions.

Limits and version notes

  • Version conflict: v1, dated 28 November 2025, reports 120 tasks, 1,026 scenarios, and 11.22% for GPT-5. Version 2 reports 145 instances, 558 scenarios, and 17.76% for GPT-5.2. These values describe different paper and benchmark snapshots. This page uses only v2 results.
  • Automatic judge error: question-level human agreement is 84% for the Product Verifier and 75.6% for the Report Verifier. A metric value is therefore not a direct human label.
  • Judge sensitivity: absolute SoP values change when the judge model changes. The three-model ranking stays stable in the reported ablation, but that ablation does not include every evaluated system or every metric.
  • Live-web drift: prices, availability, product pages, and search results can change. The paper uses verified evidence, but a later replication can see a different web environment.
  • Filtered difficulty: the authors remove questions that most evaluated agents solve and questions with more than 10 valid products. Reported results apply to the retained hard set, not to all shopping requests.
  • Category concentration: 83 of 145 instances are in Beauty and Personal Care or Electronics. Five categories contain six or fewer instances.
  • No inferential test: the paper reports means and standard deviations for five API-model runs. It does not report confidence intervals or statistical significance tests.
  • Deep Research comparison: researchers collect Deep Research answers manually from product interfaces. Those rows do not show repeated-run standard deviations, so robustness cannot be compared in the same way.
  • Model-list detail: Appendix D gives dated identifiers for OpenAI and DeepSeek API models. It lists Grok-4 in Table 2 but does not provide a dated Grok-4 identifier in the detailed model list.
  • Deployment scope: the benchmark tests recommendations and reports. It does not test payment, consent, order execution, delivery, returns, fraud, or long-term user outcomes.
