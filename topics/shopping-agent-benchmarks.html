Shopping and e-commerce agent benchmarks

Benchmarks and evaluation environments that measure how well LLM-based agents can search, compare, personalize, and purchase products in realistic e-commerce settings.

Definition

This field builds benchmarks that test autonomous agents on shopping tasks rather than static NLP tasks. The defining work is WebShop Yao et al., 2023. It frames online shopping as a sequential decision-making problem where an agent reads a natural-language instruction, navigates search, results, and item pages, selects options, and completes a purchase. WebShop pairs 1.18 million real Amazon products with crowdsourced instructions and an automatically computable reward, so evaluation needs no human in the loop Yao et al., 2023. That automatic-reward design is what makes the benchmark scalable, and most later e-commerce benchmarks inherit the same instruction-to-purchase task shape.

A central design axis is realism versus reproducibility. Offline simulated environments give controlled, repeatable conditions but a fixed slice of product diversity, while live-website evaluation gives realism at the cost of non-stationary layouts and inventories Savadikar et al., 2026. ShopGym Savadikar et al., 2026 names this tradeoff explicitly and tries to occupy the middle ground by synthesizing anonymized sandbox shops from live storefronts. DeepShop Lyu et al., 2025 takes the opposite side, evaluating agents directly on live e-commerce sites to capture dynamic real-world content.

A second axis is task complexity. WebShop instructions specify a target product plus a few attributes, options, and a price cap Yao et al., 2023. Later benchmarks argue this is too simple. DeepShop evolves queries through progressive enhancement of product attributes, search filters, and sorting preferences, producing queries far longer than earlier online benchmarks Lyu et al., 2025. ShoppingBench grounds instructions in real product catalogs and organizes them into intent categories such as Products Finder, Knowledge, Multi-products Seller, and Coupon and Budget Wang et al., 2025. WebMall adds multi-shop comparison shopping, requiring agents to search and compare offers across several heterogeneous shops Peeters et al., 2025.

A third axis is what counts as success. Many benchmarks measure task success or product-relevance against ground truth, but several add finer dimensions. ShopperBench scores persona fidelity and path consistency alongside task success Ling et al., 2025. AgenticShop uses a checklist-driven personalization score grounded in verifiable product information Kim et al., 2026. ShoppingComp adds rubric-based safety-critical decision making and reasoning faithfulness to retrieval accuracy Tou et al., 2025.

State of the area (synthesis)

The dominant finding across this field is that current agents perform well below humans on realistic shopping tasks. WebShop set the template: its best IL+RL agent reached 29% success against 59% for human experts, with option selection the primary bottleneck Yao et al., 2023. Every later benchmark that raises task realism reproduces a large gap. On DeepShop, the strongest web agent reached 32% overall success and dropped toward zero on hard multi-constraint queries Lyu et al., 2025. On ShoppingBench, the best untrained agent (GPT-4.1) stayed below 50% success Wang et al., 2025. On WebMall, frontier models completed under 65% of tasks in the hardest categories Peeters et al., 2025. ShoppingComp reports the starkest gap: state-of-the-art GPT-5.2 reached only 17.76% answer-match F1 against 30.02% for human experts Tou et al., 2025.

The benchmarks agree on where agents break down, which gives the field a shared diagnosis. The first recurring failure is search and constraint satisfaction. WebShop agents struggle to formulate and reformulate queries and to match noisy product text Yao et al., 2023. ShoppingComp finds models lean on semantic expansion with weak filtering, producing recall above precision, the opposite of the constraint-first strategy humans use Tou et al., 2025. DeepShop shows agents can satisfy attributes, filters, or sorting in isolation but rarely all at once Lyu et al., 2025. The second recurring failure is memory and replanning. WebShop agents lack the explicit memory humans use to compare products and backtrack Yao et al., 2023, and DeepShop attributes failures partly to an inability to assess state and reformulate strategy Lyu et al., 2025. Shopping Companion isolates this directly by burying preference evidence as a needle within 15 to 50 turn conversation histories; even GPT-5 reaches only 64.5% success, with unverified attributes and preference hallucination as dominant error modes Yu et al., 2026.

Grounding modality is a settled empirical question within this collection. Structural grounding through accessibility trees beats vision-only observation. WebMall reports that the accessibility tree is most important for task completion and that screenshots alone are insufficient, with vision-only Qwen agents failing every task group Peeters et al., 2025. DeepShop similarly finds vision-based approaches fail on visually dominant Fashion and Sports categories Lyu et al., 2025. This connects the topic to web-agent-environments, where VisualWebArena and WebArena study the same observation-space tradeoffs.

Personalization is an emerging sub-strand that splits success into task completion and preference alignment. ShopperBench conditions tasks on ten shopper personas grounded in consumer-behavior theory and finds a trade-off: a task-focused policy gets the highest success rate but the lowest persona alignment, while tighter persona constraints improve behavioral fidelity at the cost of longer trajectories Ling et al., 2025. AgenticShop evaluates personalized curation across the open web and finds good product search does not translate into good personalization; systems handle brand preferences but fail on review and price sensitivity, and roughly 20% of product references are hallucinated Kim et al., 2026. Shopping Companion adds the cross-session dimension, requiring agents to recover long-term preferences before recommending Yu et al., 2026. Across all three, agents remain markedly less efficient than humans, with ShopperBench reporting humans need 62.5% fewer steps Ling et al., 2025.

Two benchmarks extend the field beyond product search into adjacent commerce tasks. ECom-Bench evaluates multimodal agents on e-commerce customer support, including order modification, returns, and logistics, using persona-driven user simulation and a pass^k robustness metric; even GPT-4o reaches only 44% pass^1 and instability across repeated trials is the headline result Wang et al., 2025. This places it close to tool-agent-user-interaction, where τ-bench and τ²-Bench formalize the same tool-agent-user setup that ECom-Bench and several shopping benchmarks build on. ShoppingComp pushes the other adjacent direction, adding safety-critical decision making where agents must recognize product hazards; safety pass rates stay below 40% across all systems, with high variance Tou et al., 2025.

The field is now self-reflective about its own evaluation methodology. ShopGym formalizes a five-property target for benchmarks, realistic, diverse, controllable, inspectable, and reproducible, and validates that agent performance on synthetic twin shops tracks performance on the live storefronts they were derived from Savadikar et al., 2026. Several benchmarks adopt rubric-based or checklist-based LLM-as-judge evaluation to make scoring fine-grained and verifiable Tou et al., 2025 Kim et al., 2026 Lyu et al., 2025. A parallel methodological move is training, not just evaluating: ShoppingBench distills GPT-4.1 trajectories into a 4B model that matches it Wang et al., 2025, and Shopping Companion introduces annotation-free tool-wise rewards that let a 4B agent approach GPT-5 Yu et al., 2026. Both results suggest that closing the human gap may come from better process-level supervision rather than larger models alone.

Papers in this field

  • Yao et al., 2023 — WebShop, a simulated e-commerce environment with 1.18M real products and automatic rewards for training and evaluating grounded language agents that shop on the web.
  • Lyu et al., 2025 — DeepShop, a benchmark of progressively complex live-website shopping queries that evolve along product attributes, search filters, and sorting preferences.
  • Wang et al., 2025 — ShoppingBench, a 3,310-instruction intent-grounded benchmark over 2.5M real products with intent-specific metrics and a trajectory-distillation training strategy.
  • Savadikar et al., 2026 — ShopGym, a framework that synthesizes anonymized sandbox shops from live storefronts and grounded tasks, bridging live realism and sandbox reproducibility.
  • Ling et al., 2025 — ShopperBench, a persona-conditioned shopping benchmark that measures persona fidelity and path consistency alongside task success.
  • Tou et al., 2025 — ShoppingComp, an expert-curated open-world benchmark covering multi-constraint retrieval, expert-level report generation, and safety-critical decision making.
  • Kim et al., 2026 — AgenticShop, the first benchmark for personalized product curation across the open web with a checklist-driven personalization evaluation grounded in verifiable product information.
  • Yu et al., 2026 — Shopping Companion, a long-horizon cross-session benchmark requiring agents to recover long-term preferences, plus annotation-free tool-wise rewards for training lightweight agents.
  • Wang et al., 2025 — ECom-Bench, the first open-source benchmark for multimodal LLM agents with MCP tools in e-commerce customer support, using persona-driven user simulation.
  • Peeters et al., 2025 — WebMall, the first offline reproducible multi-shop benchmark requiring comparison shopping across heterogeneous shops populated with real Common Crawl offers.

Contradictions

  • None directly flagged among collected papers. The benchmarks broadly agree on the human-agent gap and on accessibility-tree grounding over vision-only observation; differences are in scope and design (offline versus live, single-shop versus multi-shop, task-success versus personalization-aware scoring) rather than conflicting empirical claims.

Open questions / gaps

  • Agents cannot reliably satisfy multiple constraints (attributes, filters, sorting) simultaneously despite competence on each in isolation Lyu et al., 2025 Tou et al., 2025 Yao et al., 2023. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Agents lack explicit memory and replanning for comparing products, backtracking, and recovering long-term preferences across sessions Yao et al., 2023 Lyu et al., 2025 Yu et al., 2026. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Multimodal grounding remains weak: vision-only agents fail and effective fusion of HTML and visual signals is unsolved, especially for visually dominant product categories Peeters et al., 2025 Lyu et al., 2025. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Safety-critical shopping behavior is unreliable, with the strongest systems passing under 40% of safety-trap rubrics and showing unstable, non-deterministic safety outcomes Tou et al., 2025. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Personalization does not follow from search competence; agents handle brand preferences but fail on review and price sensitivity, and persona alignment trades off against task efficiency Kim et al., 2026 Ling et al., 2025. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Agents are markedly less efficient than humans, using many more steps and high token and runtime cost on multi-shop and long-horizon tasks Ling et al., 2025 Peeters et al., 2025 Yu et al., 2026. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Output instability across repeated trials remains high, with pass^k dropping sharply below pass^1 even for the strongest models Wang et al., 2025. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Benchmark coverage is incomplete: login-protected flows, checkout and payment, customer-service interactions, multilingual settings, and post-purchase workflows are under-tested Savadikar et al., 2026 Wang et al., 2025 Tou et al., 2025. Mirrored in open-questions.
