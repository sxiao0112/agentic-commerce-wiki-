Bittensor Agent Arenas as a Trajectory Primitive: Distilling a Shopping Agent from ShoppingBench Subnet Traces

Contribution

The authors argue that a deliberately engineered incentive-aligned agent arena can be engineered as a trajectory-data generator for small-model agentic post-training, and demonstrate this through ORO Subnet 15 (SN15), a Bittensor deployment of the ShoppingBench benchmark. They characterize Bittensor agent subnets as a new training-data primitive with three structural properties: incentive-aligned diversity from continuous independent competition, per-trajectory judging by an LLM reasoning judge, and rotating held-out problems guarded against memorization. The authors propose a structural-quality filter to convert the raw subnet trajectory firehose into a trainable corpus by selecting agentic trajectories (where the language model itself emits tool calls) and rejecting sub-task trajectories (where tool calls are deterministically generated). They post-train Qwen3-4B on the filtered subnet-generated corpus using a five-stage pipeline closely matched to the published ShoppingBench SFT-then-GRPO recipe, achieving a 24.7-point ASR gain on a leak-cluster-guarded held-out partition.

Research Question

Can a Bittensor subnet specifically engineered as an agent arena generate the right shape of judged multi-turn trajectories for small-model agentic post-training, and can those trajectories post-train a small open model into a competent shopping agent?

Method

SN15 combines three mechanisms to produce trainable trajectories. The race mechanism sustains discover-then-share pressure through daily promotion, twelve-hour submission cooldowns, and source-code embargo: many independent teams are continuously paid to discover policies competitors have not found. An LLM reasoning judge independently scores each trajectory on reasoning quality, assigning a coefficient that multiplies outcome scores before aggregation. Rotating held-out problems with leak-cluster guards group paraphrased or attribute-reordered variants of the same underlying problem to defeat surface-form memorization. The structural-quality filter has three stages: a reasoning-coefficient gate drops trajectories below a fixed quality threshold, a format-validity gate hard-rejects malformed traces on five structural invariants, and a deduplication and ranking pass picks one to two trajectories per problem by structural-quality signals (think-and-tool-call depth, search-query reformulation count, verification-step presence, step-shape regularity). An axis-A versus axis-B split retains only agentic trajectories in which the language model itself emits tool calls; axis-B trajectories in which tool calls are deterministically generated are filtered out. The post-training pipeline proceeds in five stages on Qwen3-4B: one-epoch LoRA SFT on the structural-filter corpus, rejection-sampled re-SFT with k=8 rollouts, continued teacher-SFT on Sonnet 4.6 teacher rollouts (substituting step-wise OPD), KTO preference refinement, and Dr. GRPO turn-level RL on top of the post-teacher-SFT checkpoint.

Setup

The paper evaluates agents against ShoppingBench, a multi-turn tool-use benchmark where agents are given natural-language shopping intents and scored on whether their final recommendation satisfies price, service, SKU, and attribute constraints. The main evaluation is on a 75-problem leak-cluster-guarded held-out partition scored production-strict through a 7-tool ORO ShoppingBench harness. The base model is Qwen3-4B. Training uses common configuration with one epoch LoRA, learning rate sweep over {1e-5, 3e-5, 1e-4}, max-length 14336, SDPA attention, and gradient checkpointing. Rejection-sampled re-SFT samples k=8 rollouts per problem at temperature 0.9. Teacher trajectories come from running Claude Sonnet 4.6 through held-out evaluation on a 498-problem race-bank training set, with roughly 210 scoring under production-strict. KTO uses β=0.02, learning rate 5e-6, one epoch. Dr. GRPO v19 (the reported variant) uses B=8, learning rate 5e-7, β=0.2, with a blended outcome-plus-tool-match reward where the tool-match component grades policy per-step choices against a teacher trajectory.

Results

On the 75-problem leak-cluster-guarded held-out partition, Qwen3-4B base scores 18.0% ASR. The ORO SFT stack lifts this to 42.7%, approaching the published synthetic-data SFT-only baseline of 43.6% on the same model. The published Qwen3-4B SFT+GRPO bar stands at 48.7%. The SFT stack has measurable pass@k headroom: pass@8=53.3% versus pass@1=34.8% on the held-out partition. KTO refinement on top of the SFT stack produces no net overall lift (both score 32 of 75 problems) but redistributes probability mass within buckets: voucher ASR rises by 4.3 percentage points, product ASR falls by 3.6 percentage points, shop ASR is flat. Dr. GRPO v19 demonstrates process improvement within twenty optimization steps: in-training rule score lifts from 0.02 to 0.42, hallucination count falls from fourteen to zero, partial successes rise from zero to twenty-four of forty-eight on a held-out diagnostic. However, strict exact-match success rate remains zero across all twenty steps. The dominant constraint on the headline number is the axis-A versus axis-B data skew: the daily SN15 firehose (12,000 to 27,000 trajectories per day) is order-of-magnitude dominated by axis-B trajectories, of which only the small minority come from agentic baselines or direct forks.

Conclusion

Bittensor agent subnets, when deliberately engineered with race mechanics, per-trajectory LLM judging, and rotating held-out problems, produce a structurally distinct shape of training data for agentic post-training that differs from both frontier-synthesized data (which collapses long-tail diversity) and unfiltered production logs (which are unjudged and contaminated by shortcuts). The SN15 subnet produces trajectories with incentive-aligned diversity, per-trajectory reasoning-quality supervision, and genuine held-out evaluation. A small-model agentic post-training pipeline can be matched to such data: the structural-quality filter successfully converts the raw firehose into a trainable corpus, and the filtered corpus successfully post-trains Qwen3-4B to match the published SFT-only baseline performance using a small fraction of a single day of subnet output. The primary leverage for closing the remaining gap to the published SFT+GRPO bar lies on the data side: bringing the order-of-magnitude-larger axis-B firehose into training via corpus-side rewriting or subnet-side incentive rebalancing.

Related Work

Agentic post-training corpora

ToolLLM and ToolBench: Introduced large-scale API tool-use SFT data generated by frontier-model role-play — one-shot batch generation, outcome-only supervision, offline held-out construction.

ToolACE: Adds self-evolution loop to frontier-model synthesis.

Magpie: Generalizes the synthetic-data recipe to alignment data.

Tulu 3: Demonstrates RLVR with external verifiers outperforms recipes without per-trajectory verification on synthetic data.

DeepSeek-R1: Demonstrates that verifier-grounded RL materially outperforms recipes that do not include an external verifier.

Model collapse literature (Shumailov et al.): Published research showing pure self-distillation reliably collapses long-tail data distribution before degrading aggregate quality.

Production-trajectory post-training in vertical coding agents

Cursor Composer 2.5: Post-trains open base with targeted SFT and on-policy RL against rollouts in controlled coding environment; feeds successful RL trajectories back into SFT pool.

Cognition SWE-1.5: Matched recipe with three grading mechanisms (test suites, code-quality rubrics, agentic grading via browser testing) in place of single outcome score; explicitly emphasizes that production trajectories, not synthetic data, drive the recipe.

Commerce and shopping agents

ShoppingBench: Established the Qwen3-4B SFT-then-GRPO recipe and published a synthetic GPT-4.1 trajectory corpus; the baseline this work measures itself against.

Shopify Flow: Published engineering write-up reporting matched recipe (synthetic trajectories plus LLM judge); explicitly acknowledges offline benchmarks mask substantial real-world failure modes.

Amazon Rufus: Described in public communications as retrieval-augmented shopping assistant; no published methodology.

Incentivised and decentralised data generation

LMSYS Chatbot Arena: Crowdsources continuous head-to-head preference judgements; pairwise preference supervision rather than multi-turn judged trajectory; uncompensated population.

Bittensor sibling subnets: Run continuous competitions on different task surfaces (inference-cost minimisation, pretraining races, crowdsourced annotation); produce data shapes not directly usable as agent trajectories.
