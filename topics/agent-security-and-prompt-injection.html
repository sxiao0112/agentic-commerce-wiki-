Agent Security and Prompt Injection

How autonomous LLM agents are attacked through the content they read, whether the defenses meant to stop those attacks hold up in realistic settings, and how an agent's own behavior might be monitored at runtime.

Definition

Prompt injection is the central security threat in this field. Malicious instructions placed in content the agent reads can hijack its behavior, divert it from the user task, or leak data. Li et al., 2026 frames the deployment-facing version of this problem: an agent security defense must block injected attacker goals while still letting the agent complete open-ended user tasks.

Li et al., 2026 organizes existing defenses into four categories. Prompting-based defenses rewrite the prompt to keep the model focused on the original user instruction, for example by repeating trusted instructions after each tool call or by delimiting untrusted tool outputs. Filtering-based defenses use a separate classifier to detect and remove injection attempts in external data. Alignment-based defenses fine-tune the model itself to prioritize the user query over instructions found in external content. System-level defenses constrain execution through tool isolation, information-flow control, or dynamic privilege policies.

A defense is judged on both security and utility. Li et al., 2026 reports three metrics: benign utility (task completion with no attack), utility under attack, and attack success rate (the fraction of cases where the attacker's goal succeeds). The failure mode opposite to insecurity is overdefense, where a defense suppresses attacks but also blocks the benign instructions an agent needs to finish its task.

The injection an agent reads has to reach the agent first, and for retrieval-augmented agents the attacker can target that delivery channel directly. Ben-Tov & Sharif, 2025 studies corpus poisoning against dense embedding-based retrievers, the search component that many agents and RAG systems use to fetch context. A corpus-poisoning attack inserts crafted adversarial passages into the searchable corpus at inference time, without retraining the model, so that they rank highly and are retrieved for chosen queries. This is the upstream surface on which an injected payload then rides into the agent's context.

The defenses above all act on what the agent reads. A separate line watches what the agent does. Sims, 2026 studies activation-based behavioral monitoring: a runtime monitor that detects strategic deception by reading the agent's own residual-stream activations rather than its inputs. The probe is a linear classifier trained on residual-stream activations to decode whether the agent is being deceptive on a given turn. This is a behavioral defense layer rather than an injection defense, and it targets misbehavior that originates inside the agent rather than in the content it reads.

State of the area (synthesis)

The wiki holds three papers here. Two trace one attack path: getting malicious content in front of the agent, and getting the agent to obey it. The third opens a separate defense layer that watches the agent's own behavior at runtime.

The downstream stage of the attack path is defense evaluation. Li et al., 2026 observes that defenses now reach near-perfect scores on current benchmarks, yet argues this hides a gap between benchmark results and real-world deployment. Its contribution is AgentDyn, a manually designed benchmark built to expose that gap. AgentDyn differs from prior benchmarks along three deliberate axes. Tasks require dynamic planning, so the agent must adapt as the environment responds rather than follow a fixed script. Each task embeds at least one benign, necessary helpful instruction in the critical path, so a defense cannot simply discard all external instructions. Tasks are longer and span multiple integrated applications, with trajectories of roughly six to nine steps across two to four application scenarios. The three suites cover Shopping, GitHub, and Daily Life across seven application types, combining 60 user tasks with 28 injection tasks into 560 security test cases. The threat model places injections in realistic surfaces such as advertisement banners rather than direct page manipulation.

The empirical result is that defenses which look solved are not. Li et al., 2026 evaluates ten state-of-the-art defenses across twelve commercial and open-source agents and finds that almost all either lack security or suffer severe overdefense. Filtering-based defenses struggle to separate helpful instructions from malicious ones. Planning-dependent methods lose utility sharply in dynamic scenarios. The strongest defense in the study, the alignment-based Meta SecAlign, reaches only 53.4% utility on AgentDyn versus 80% on the prior AgentDojo benchmark. The takeaway is a mismatch between benchmark conditions and deployment conditions.

The upstream stage is the retrieval channel itself. Ben-Tov & Sharif, 2025 shows that the dense retrievers feeding agent context are highly vulnerable to corpus poisoning. Its attack, GASLITE, is a gradient-based method that optimizes an adversarial passage to maximize embedding similarity with a target query distribution, without access to the corpus content or the retriever weights. The poisoning rates needed are negligible. Concept-specific attacks succeed with about ten inserted passages, roughly 0.0001% of an 8.8M-passage corpus, exceeding 50% average appeared@10 for most of nine evaluated retrievers. Single-query attacks are fully solved. The susceptibility varies sharply by model, which Ben-Tov & Sharif, 2025 traces to embedding space anisotropy and the choice of similarity metric. The same mechanism the authors use to promote content is the one indirect prompt injection rides into RAG systems, so retrieval robustness is a precondition for any downstream injection defense to matter.

The two attack-path papers also share a methodological complaint: prior work made its target look safer than it is. Li et al., 2026 argues benchmarks understate the threat by testing static, single-application tasks. Ben-Tov & Sharif, 2025 argues prior retrieval-attack evaluations understated vulnerability by using oversimplified single-query threat models and weak baselines, and both call for worst-case evaluation under realistic threat models.

Behavioral monitoring is the complementary defense layer, watching the agent rather than its inputs. Sims, 2026 trains linear probes on residual-stream activations from three instruction-tuned models and detects strategic deception in two-agent commercial negotiation at AUC above 0.93 on 11 of 12 model-scenario combinations. The deception signal concentrates in a single mass-mean direction; projecting that direction out collapses AUC by 0.30 to 0.44, so the bulk of the signal is one-dimensional. The deployment case is cheap monitoring: a probe trained on explicit instructed deception detects emergent, incentive-driven deception in the same model at AUC 0.70 to 0.77, so synthetic instructed examples can train a monitor for the harder emergent case. This matters for agentic commerce because the scenarios are negotiation settings where an agent is rewarded for misrepresenting a walkaway price, professing loyalty while planning defection, or withholding a known defect. A behavioral monitor here is a defense against agent misbehavior in transactions, distinct from the input-side injection defenses Li et al., 2026 evaluates.

The honest limit of this monitoring layer is portability across architectures. Sims, 2026 finds within-model-family transfer strong but cross-family transfer weak. A direction trained on Gemma with theory-of-mind context projects onto the same model without that context at AUC 0.918, but cross-family transfer between Llama and Mistral collapses to 0.51 to 0.56. The practical guidance is to recalibrate per architecture rather than assume a probe ports. The paper also flags that it establishes correlation, not behavioral causality: logit-level steering shows a monotone dose-response, but generation-loop steering to confirm that the direction drives more deceptive text is left as the immediate next experiment. The deception studied is also internal misbehavior under incentive, not an injected attacker goal, so this layer addresses a threat the injection defenses do not, and vice versa.

This security work connects to the broader wiki. The corpus-poisoning channel is the adversarial counterpart of the ranking-manipulation studied in generative-engine-optimization, where the goal is promoting chosen content into model-mediated retrieval. The realism axis AgentDyn pushes on mirrors concerns in web-agent-environments about whether agent environments capture real interactive dynamics. Its Shopping suite situates injection threats inside the same purchasing workflows studied in shopping-agent-benchmarks, drawing its environment design partly from WebArena. The deception-monitoring layer sits beside agent-to-agent-negotiation, where the negotiating behaviors it watches are studied as a capability rather than a risk.

Papers in this field

  • Li et al., 2026 — AgentDyn, a benchmark showing that state-of-the-art prompt-injection defenses fail on realistic dynamic agent tasks despite near-perfect scores on existing benchmarks.
  • Ben-Tov & Sharif, 2025 — GASLITE, a gradient-based corpus-poisoning attack showing that dense embedding-based retrievers feeding agents and RAG systems are highly vulnerable to adversarial passages at negligible poisoning rates.
  • Sims, 2026 — linear probes on residual-stream activations detect strategic deception in multi-agent negotiation across three model families, validating cheap within-family runtime monitors but revealing that cross-architecture transfer collapses.

Open questions / gaps

  • AgentDyn still cannot fully capture the complexity and diversity of real-world environments, so more realistic evaluation frameworks remain an open need Li et al., 2026. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Attack development lags behind defense development; the benchmark's existing attacks are relatively weak against frontier models such as GPT-5 and Claude-4, and building stronger, more realistic attack frameworks is identified as an important complementary direction Li et al., 2026. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Retrieval-poisoning robustness is largely unaddressed by defenses; Ben-Tov & Sharif, 2025 explores only L2-norm and perplexity filtering and assumes white-box, BERT-base-sized open-source retrievers evaluated mainly on MSMARCO and NQ, leaving proprietary, larger-scale, and specialized-domain retrievers open. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Activation-based deception monitors are validated only for correlation, not behavioral causality; generation-loop steering to confirm the direction drives more deceptive text is left as the immediate next experiment, and cross-architecture transfer is weak beyond a single model family at 7-8B scale Sims, 2026. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Whether activation-based deception monitoring generalizes beyond commercial negotiation to social manipulation, cooperative-task betrayal, and long-horizon planning is untested Sims, 2026. Mirrored in open-questions.
