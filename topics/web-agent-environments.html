Realistic web environments for autonomous agents

Self-hosted and simulated web environments, with executable tasks and automatic reward functions, for training and evaluating agents that browse, search, and transact like humans.

Definition

A web environment is a navigable set of websites an agent acts on through browser-like actions, paired with tasks and an automatic check for whether each task actually succeeded. Zhou et al., 2024 builds WebArena as a fully functional, self-hosted environment of four websites spanning e-commerce, a social forum, collaborative software development, and content management, enriched with utility tools and knowledge resources. Zhou et al., 2024 evaluates by functional correctness, checking whether the intended outcome is reached rather than whether the action sequence matches a reference. Tasks that are unachievable are included, and agents are scored on whether they appropriately refuse rather than hallucinate an answer Zhou et al., 2024.

The observation an agent sees is a design choice. Zhou et al., 2024 represents pages as accessibility trees with element IDs, so agents select elements by ID. Koh et al., 2024 extends this to visual grounding with VisualWebArena, 910 tasks where visual understanding is necessary rather than supplementary. Koh et al., 2024 introduces a Set-of-Marks observation that automatically annotates every interactable element with a bounding box and a unique ID, and shows it improves vision-language models on visually dense sites.

The earliest large-scale environment in this collection is WebShop. Yao et al., 2023 builds a simulated e-commerce site of 1.18 million real products scraped from Amazon, with 12,087 crowdsourced shopping instructions and an automatically computable reward over attributes, options, price, and product type. Yao et al., 2023 avoids expensive human-in-the-loop evaluation by making the reward programmatic, and demonstrates sim-to-real transfer by deploying WebShop-trained agents directly on Amazon and eBay without fine-tuning.

Two newer environments target reproducible, controllable evaluation. Peeters et al., 2025 builds WebMall, the first offline multi-shop benchmark, with four electronics shops populated from real Common Crawl product offers and 91 comparison-shopping tasks. Savadikar et al., 2026 builds ShopGym, a framework that converts live storefronts into anonymized sandbox shops through specification synthesis and staged code generation, then synthesizes grounded tasks validated against the generated shops.

State of the area (synthesis)

The defining tension in this area is realism versus reproducibility. Live websites are diverse and authentic but non-stationary and irreproducible; sandboxes are controllable but capture only a narrow slice of real e-commerce Savadikar et al., 2026. Each environment in this collection picks a point on that spectrum. Yao et al., 2023 and Zhou et al., 2024 both self-host realistic content while keeping evaluation deterministic and automatic. Zhou et al., 2024 frames the gap it fills as the oversimplification, lack of dynamic interaction, and surface-form scoring of earlier environments.

Functional, execution-based reward is now the shared evaluation paradigm. Yao et al., 2023 computes reward from matched attributes, options, and price. Zhou et al., 2024 inspects intermediate execution states such as databases, URLs, and page content to verify outcomes. Koh et al., 2024 adds visual reward primitives, including vision-language question answering and structural image similarity, so open-ended visual tasks can be checked automatically. This lineage moves the field away from action-sequence matching toward did-the-task-succeed scoring.

Observation modality is a recurring axis of comparison. Koh et al., 2024 finds multimodal agents substantially outperform text-only agents on visually grounded tasks, and that Set-of-Marks helps the strongest vision-language model on dense sites. Peeters et al., 2025 reports a complementary result for comparison shopping. There the accessibility tree is most important for task completion, screenshots alone are insufficient, and vision-only agents can fail entirely when they cannot map rendered pixels to navigation elements. Read together, these suggest vision helps when tasks are inherently visual, but structural grounding remains essential for reliable navigation Koh et al., 2024 Peeters et al., 2025.

A large and persistent agent-human gap runs through every environment. WebShop agents reach 29% success against 59% for human experts Yao et al., 2023. WebArena's best agent reaches 11.7% against 78.2% for humans Zhou et al., 2024. VisualWebArena's best agent reaches 16.4% against 88.7% for humans Koh et al., 2024. On the multi-shop tasks of Peeters et al., 2025, frontier models stay below 65% completion on the hardest categories of cheapest-product and vague-product search. The shared failure modes are concrete. Agents struggle with search-query reformulation, option selection, exploration, and long-horizon memory Yao et al., 2023. They show observation bias and inconsistency across task variants of the same template Zhou et al., 2024. They loop between pages and abandon tasks too early Koh et al., 2024.

The newest work shifts from authoring one fixed environment to generating environments at scale. Savadikar et al., 2026 grounds generation in observed real shops, writing an anonymized specification during an exploration phase, then synthesizing calibrated sandbox shops and validated tasks from it. Savadikar et al., 2026 reports that agent success on synthetic twins positively tracks success on the live storefronts they imitate, and that fully synthetic composite shops yield benchmarks of comparable difficulty. This is a methodological answer to the realism-reproducibility tension rather than a new website, and it positions environment construction itself as a research target.

These environments are the substrate the rest of agentic commerce evaluates against. The e-commerce environments here, especially WebShop and WebMall, anchor the broader space of shopping-agent-benchmarks, while the visual and multi-shop observation findings inform tool-agent-user-interaction and the simulated-market work in agentic-marketplaces. Embodied store simulation in embodied-retail-environments mirrors the same realism-versus-control tradeoff in a physical rather than web setting.

Papers in this field

  • Yao et al., 2023 — WebShop, a large-scale simulated e-commerce environment of 1.18M real products with grounded language instructions, automatic reward, and demonstrated sim-to-real transfer to Amazon and eBay.
  • Zhou et al., 2024 — WebArena, a fully functional self-hosted environment of four realistic websites with 812 long-horizon tasks scored by functional correctness.
  • Koh et al., 2024 — VisualWebArena, 910 visually grounded web tasks for multimodal agents, with a Set-of-Marks observation representation that improves vision-language model grounding.
  • Peeters et al., 2025 — WebMall, the first offline multi-shop benchmark, with four shops of authentic Common Crawl offers and 91 comparison-shopping tasks across heterogeneous interfaces.
  • Savadikar et al., 2026 — ShopGym, a framework that synthesizes anonymized sandbox shops from live storefronts and generates grounded, validated tasks, bridging live-web realism and sandbox control.

Open questions / gaps

  • Agents remain far below human performance on realistic web tasks, with search-query reformulation, option selection, exploration, and long-horizon memory the persistent bottlenecks Yao et al., 2023 Zhou et al., 2024 Koh et al., 2024. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Vision-only observation is insufficient for reliable navigation, and how to combine visual grounding with structural grounding for fine-grained web text remains open Koh et al., 2024 Peeters et al., 2025. Mirrored in open-questions.
  • Reconciling live-website realism with reproducible, controllable evaluation is unresolved, and automatically generated environments still need broader coverage of login flows, checkout and payment, multi-session memory, and safety-critical edge cases Savadikar et al., 2026.
